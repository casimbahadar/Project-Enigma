# Cleave Studios — Release Strategy

How the slate ships and earns. Treats Steam-first and mobile-port-later
as a *single* dual-platform decision, not two separate ones.

## Lead title — locked

**Ember & Echo** is the lead. Two reasons it stands even though it is
not the strictly fastest title to ship:

- **Solo-dev motivation compounds.** The fastest game to ship is the
  one that gets finished. Founder excitement on Ember & Echo is the
  productivity multiplier that beats raw scope speed.
- **Scope is predictable, not minimum.** Per-Echo writing budget is
  bounded (one-screen vignettes); chapter-as-emotion structure caps
  content; FE-style grid combat is well-trodden. Easier to estimate
  honestly than the writing-bottomless *Lasting* or
  engineering-bottomless *Grafted*.

## Sequencing — full slate

**Ember & Echo → Grafted → The Lasting → Chorus → Constellate**

| # | Title | Why this slot |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ember & Echo | Lead. Builds: FE-grid engine, vignettes, supports, branching evolution. |
| 2 | **Grafted** | Strongest dual-platform fit on the slate. Mobile-native sharing model. Procedural-sprite engineering becomes a `shared/` asset that de-risks Constellate. |
| 3 | The Lasting | Recovery title — light mechanical investment after Grafted's heavy build. Compounds writing chops. Mobile-friendly text/ATB. |
| 4 | Chorus | Studio is now multi-shipped; can absorb the audio engineering risk. Steam-strong, mobile-tolerable. |
| 5 | Constellate | Capstone flagship. Maximum engine maturity + audience size + procedural-sprite toolkit available by this point. |

This sequence trades a slightly slower #2 release (Grafted is more
engine work than Chorus would be) for two strategic assets:

- **Larger audience expansion at #2.** Grafted's procedural-roster
  sharing is mobile-native organic marketing — TikTok/Reels reach
  Chorus cannot match on a silenced phone.
- **Capstone de-risking.** Constellate ships 36+ named creatures
  (12 constellations × creature families). A procedural sprite-
  composition system built in Grafted ports to Constellate's content
  pipeline directly.

## Mobile monetization realities

Mobile users expect free-with-IAP. The studio's locked premium
one-time + paid chapter expansions model (no gacha, no FOMO, no
live-ops) is harder on mobile than Steam. Workable paths within the
constraint:

- **$4.99–$9.99 premium price on mobile.** Works for known indie
  devs; requires wishlist momentum from Steam to justify.
- **Free first chapter, paid subsequent chapters.** Legitimate "demo"
  model; fits the chapter-expansion plan; lowers mobile friction.
- **Full premium + paid DLC chapters.** Maps to plan exactly; lower
  conversion rate but higher per-conversion revenue.

**Steam-first ordering is intentional.** Steam launches build the
wishlist, review base, and word-of-mouth that justify a $9.99 mobile
premium price. Mobile-first launches without that backing typically
have to compete on free.

## Revenue checkpoints — pull-forward levers

The default plan path to first revenue is roughly month 13–14 from
Phase 0 (full Steam launch of Episode 1). The following levers within
the locked plan can pull revenue earlier:

1. **Sell the polished V-slice as a paid itch.io demo** at $5–10
   around month 6, before Steam launch. The demo exists anyway per
   Phase 4 verification — choosing paid over free captures early
   revenue from the niche audience that wants to back solo devs.
2. **Launch Episode 1 alone on Steam at full premium price** ($15–
   $20). Don't wait for full-game completion; the chapter-expansion
   model is designed for episodic launch.
3. **Treat Steam Next Fest as the wishlist driver, not the launch.**
   Plan target is 5,000+ wishlists post–Next Fest. With wishlist
   momentum, Episode 1 launch sells better than a delayed
   complete-game launch.

Revised target timeline if the paper prototype passes:

| Months | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Phase 2 paper prototypes |
| 3–5 | Phase 3 vertical slice |
| 6 | itch.io demo (paid or free) |
| 7 | Steam Next Fest |
| 7–12 | Episode 1 production |
| 13–14 | **Steam launch — Episode 1.** First major revenue. |
| 15–18 | Mobile port of Episode 1 (Steam-validated audience) |

Subsequent chapters and titles compound on this base.

## Cross-slate audience strategy

Each Steam launch builds the audience for the next title's mobile
port. Specifically:

- Ember & Echo Steam reviews → Ember & Echo mobile pricing power
- Grafted Steam release → Grafted mobile launch leverages
  established studio name; procedural-roster sharing reaches mobile
  audiences who didn't see the Steam launch
- By Constellate (#5), the studio has 4 launches of trust + 4 mobile
  audiences to invite back — capstone ships into the strongest
  possible market position
